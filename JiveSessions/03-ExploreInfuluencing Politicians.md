# International Examples of Voter Information Platforms

## Europe: Voting Advice Applications (VAAs)

### **Germany: Wahl-O-Mat** (Most established)
- **Operator**: Federal Agency for Civic Education (government-run)
- **Scale**: 6.7 million users in 2009 (12% of electorate), grew to 21 million users in 2021 election
- **How it works**: 38 policy statements, users indicate agree/neutral/disagree, matched to parties
- **Development**: Created by group of 10-25 young people including first-time voters, working with political scientists, educators, statisticians
- **Impact**: Research shows it causes measurable increase in political knowledge, specifically about parties' positions on issues
- **Interesting note**: Parties must approve their positions before inclusion

**Key Learning**: Government backing gives credibility, but they've faced legal challenges from smaller parties claiming bias in the comparison interface.

---

### **Netherlands: StemWijzer** (Pioneer)
- **Operator**: ProDemos (civic education foundation)
- **Scale**: 4.7 million consultations in 2006 (40% of electorate); 6.85 million users in week before 2017 election
- **Design**: Simple, user-friendly, three answering categories
- **Impact**: Very high penetration in a smaller country

**Key Learning**: Simplicity works. The "3 options" model (agree/neutral/disagree) is more accessible than complex scales.

---

### **Switzerland: Smartvote**
- **Operator**: Politools (non-profit)
- **Design**: More complex algorithmic methods, multiple result visualizations
- **Unique feature**: Candidate-centered (not just parties), with 2D political maps

**Key Learning**: Works well in systems with many parties and candidate-focused voting.

---

### **Finland: Multiple VAAs** (Competitive market)
- At least 13 VAAs for 2023 parliamentary elections, mostly hosted by large media houses like MTV3, which reaches over 20% of the 4.3 million electorate
- Competition between news channels
- Voters compare results across different VAAs

**Key Learning**: Media organizations successfully operate their own tools, creating a "marketplace" of perspectives.

## Research Findings on VAAs

**What works:**
- VAAs significantly increase knowledge about parties' positions on issues
- Exposure to VAA advice leads voters to update their party preferences in line with information provided
- Some evidence of increased turnout, especially among younger voters

**What doesn't work as well:**
- Little evidence of downstream effects on political behaviors like turning out to vote or changing self-reported vote choice
- Those with particularly low levels of political interest are least likely to be motivated to use VAAs

**The "empowerment paradox"**: VAAs reach people who already care about politics, struggle to engage the truly disengaged.

## United States: BallotReady

### **BallotReady** (Comprehensive local focus)
- **Founded**: 2015 by Alex Niemczewski and Aviva Rosman
- **Unique angle**: Focuses on local elections, addresses the fact that 96% of all elected officials are elected locally and up to 30% of voters leave part of ballot blank
- **How it works**: 
  - Enter address → see personalized ballot
  - Candidate bios, endorsements, issue stances, social media
  - Print or email ballot selections to bring to voting booth
- **Funding**: Knight Foundation, National Science Foundation, civic grants
- **Partners**: Snapchat, Miami Heat, Kerry Washington, Lizzo, Jonas Brothers
- **Research process**: Team makes phone calls, files FOIA requests, sends faxes to gather accurate info from thousands of local election boards

**Key Learning**: The "cheat sheet" approach is brilliant - people can research at home, then bring selections into booth. Local focus fills a real gap.

---

## United Kingdom: TheyWorkForYou

### **TheyWorkForYou** (Accountability focus)
- **Operator**: mySociety (non-profit social enterprise)
- **Running since**: 2004 (20+ years!)
- **Focus**: Parliamentary monitoring and MP voting records
- **Key features**:
  - Enter postcode → see your MP
  - Complete voting history on key topics
  - Hansard debates (searchable, permalinks)
  - Email alerts when keywords mentioned
  - API for other organizations to use data

**Impact**: 
- Parliamentary rebellion increased, attributed in part to increased visibility of MPs' voting records
- MPs appear to vote less with their party and more in interests of constituents
- Changed how parliamentarians work

**Funding**: Originally volunteers, now run by charity with donations

**Key Learning**: Track record over time is powerful. MPs changed behavior because they knew they were being watched. Long-term sustainability through charity model works.

## Common Success Factors Across All Examples

1. **Non-partisan credibility** - Government backing (Germany), academic involvement, or charity status
2. **Simple UX** - Most successful use 3-option scales, not complex surveys
3. **Visual results** - Rankings, maps, charts beat text
4. **Local relevance** - "Enter your address/postcode" personalizes experience
5. **Media partnerships** - Traditional media amplifies reach
6. **Transparency** - Source citations, methodology pages, open about limitations
7. **Mobile-first** - Essential for reaching young voters

## Common Challenges

1. **Reaching the disengaged** - Always struggle to attract low-interest voters
2. **Bias accusations** - Smaller parties often complain about algorithm/design
3. **Funding sustainability** - Ongoing operations require resources
4. **Content maintenance** - Keeping information current is labor-intensive
5. **Party cooperation** - Getting parties to submit their positions can be difficult

## Which Models Might Work Best for NZ?

**Hybrid approach combining:**
1. **VAA quiz component** (like Wahl-O-Mat/StemWijzer) - for policy matching
2. **Ballot guide** (like BallotReady) - for enrollment and candidate info
3. **Track record** (like TheyWorkForYou) - for incumbent accountability
4. **Transparency data** - your unique addition re: donors/lobbyists

**Why this could work for NZ:**
- MMP system (like Germany) makes party comparison valuable
- Size similar to Finland (scalable but not overwhelming)
- Strong civic culture (like Netherlands/Switzerland)
- Parliamentary system (TheyWorkForYou approach applicable)

**NZ-specific advantage:**
The enrollment deadline issue is a **clear, urgent hook** that these other platforms don't have. This could be your "wedge" to get attention, then the policy comparison becomes the value-add that keeps people engaged.
