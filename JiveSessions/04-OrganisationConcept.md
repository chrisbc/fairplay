Based on the international examples you provided and your specific goal of increasing transparency around political influences, here is a conceptual plan for a New Zealand-based organization.

We can call this concept **"The Public Office"** (working title). It shifts the focus from just "who to vote for" (election cycle) to "who are they working for" (continuous accountability).

### **1. Organizational Model**

To have the credibility required to "influence politicians," this organization cannot be a standard startup or a political activist group. It must be seen as a civic infrastructure provider.

*   **Legal Structure:** A non-partisan Charitable Trust or Society.
    *   *Why:* This allows tax-deductible donations and grants (like the Knight Foundation in the US or ProDemos in the Netherlands) and signals that the primary motive is public education, not profit or political power.
*   **Governance:** A board comprising constitutional lawyers, former MPs (from multiple parties), and data scientists. This "stewardship" model (like Germany’s *Bundeszentrale für politische Bildung*) provides defense against accusations of bias.

### **2. The Core Question: "Who Am I Working For?"**

This is the central narrative hook. The organization would operate on the premise that an MP has three "clients":
1.  **The Party** (The whip/voting line).
2.  **The Donor/Lobbyist** (The funding/influence).
3.  **The Electorate** (The voters).

The organization’s primary tool would visualize these competing interests to answer the question: *When the chips are down, who does this MP work for?*

### **3. Platform Features (Adapting International Examples)**

Drawing from the **TheyWorkForYou** (UK) and **BallotReady** (US) models, but adding your specific "Influence" layer:

#### **A. The "Influence Map" (The Transparency Layer)**
*   **Concept:** A visual network graph for every MP.
*   **Data Sources:**
    *   Electoral Commission (Donations returns).
    *   Register of Pecuniary Interests (MPs' personal financial interests).
    *   *New Feature:* A "Lobbyist Register" (if legislation passes) or crowdsourced/scraper-data from news reports and official disclosures.
*   **User Experience:** When a user looks at a specific Bill (e.g., "Fast-Track Approvals Bill"), they see a "Influence Overlay."
    *   *Example:* "This MP voted YES. Top 3 donors: [Industry A], [Industry B]. [Industry A] stands to benefit directly from this legislation."

#### **B. The "Mandate Tracker" (The Submission Tool)**
You mentioned "voter submissions and feedback on bills." Currently, submissions go to a Select Committee and often vanish into a PDF.
*   **The Solution:** A **"Digital Submission Portal"** integrated with the platform.
*   **How it works:**
    1.  The platform summarizes a complex Bill into plain English (like *Wahl-O-Mat*).
    2.  Users vote "Support / Oppose / Amend" and add comments.
    3.  The platform generates a formal submission PDF that the user can send directly to the Select Committee with one click.
    4.  **The "Accountability Loop":** The platform tracks how the MP voted compared to the majority of their electorate’s submissions.
    5.  **Outcome:** A "Responsiveness Score" for the MP (e.g., "MP Smith voted against 80% of their own constituents' submissions on this bill").

#### **C. The "Honesty Meter" (The Campaign Check)**
*   **Concept:** Real-time fact-checking of campaign promises vs. track records.
*   **Adaptation:** Using the *TheyWorkForYou* model, you track every promise made in the 2026 campaign.
*   **Status Updates:**
    *   ✅ **Kept:** Legislation passed.
    *   🔄 **In Progress:** Bill introduced.
    *   ❌ **Broken:** Voted against or abandoned.
    *   ⚠️ **Compromised:** Changed due to coalition agreements (highlighting the "Who am I working for?" aspect—did they blame the coalition partner?).

### **4. Strategy to Influence Politicians**

The organization itself doesn't need to lobby in the traditional sense; it creates **transparency pressure**.

*   **The "Sunlight" Effect:** As seen in the UK (*TheyWorkForYou*), MPs change behavior when they know it is being recorded and publicized. If an MP knows their "Donor Alignment Score" is public, they may be more hesitant to vote purely for donor interests.
*   **The "Transparency Pledge":** Before the election, the organization asks all candidates to sign a "Transparency Pledge":
    *   *I pledge to declare all meetings with lobbyists within 48 hours.*
    *   *I pledge to prioritize my electorate’s submission feedback.*
*   **Media Partnerships:** Like *Vote Compass* in Canada/Australia, you would partner with major NZ media (e.g., RNZ, NZ Herald, Stuff) to host the data. This ensures that when an MP acts against their constituents' interests, it becomes a news story.

### **5. Addressing the Challenges**

Using the insights from your international research:

*   **The "Empowerment Paradox":** The research shows these tools often reach those already interested.
    *   *Solution:* The "Submission Tool" solves this. It turns passive reading into active clicking. By lowering the barrier to making a submission, you capture the "busy but interested" demographic.
*   **Bias Accusations:**
    *   *Solution:* Adopt the *Wahl-O-Mat* approach. Before publishing an "Influence Map," give the MP a right of reply. "Our data shows X. Do you dispute this?" Publish their reply alongside the data.
*   **Data Maintenance:**
    *   *Solution:* Use "Civic Crowdsourcing." Allow users to submit links to news articles or disclosures about lobbyists. A small team of volunteer moderators verifies them (similar to Wikipedia editing). This reduces operational costs.

### **6. Implementation Roadmap for NZ 2026**

1.  **Phase 1 (Development):** Build the scraper for the NZ Parliament (Hansard) and Electoral Commission donations data.
2.  **Phase 2 (The Pledge):** Launch the "Who Do You Work For?" pledge campaign early 2026. Get candidates on the record.
3.  **Phase 3 (The App):** Launch the VAA (Voting Advice Application) component 3 months out from the election (the "Policy Matcher").
4.  **Phase 4 (The Watchdog):** Post-election, switch the site to "Monitor Mode." Track the winners against their promises and their donors.

This model effectively combines the **engagement** of *Wahl-O-Mat*, the **accountability** of *TheyWorkForYou*, and the **local action** of *BallotReady*, while specifically targeting the transparency gap in NZ politics.
