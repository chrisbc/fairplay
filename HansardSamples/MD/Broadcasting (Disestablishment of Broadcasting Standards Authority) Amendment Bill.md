## General policy statement
New Zealand’s broadcasting standards regime was designed in the late 1980s for a
world of a few television channels and radio stations. Today’s media environment
could not be more different. News and entertainment now flow through countless dig‐
ital, on-demand, and social platforms, many of which operate beyond the reach of the
Broadcasting Standards Authority (BSA).
The BSA’s remit has expanded far beyond its original purpose, while its relevance to
the modern media landscape has diminished. The same content rules do not apply
across platforms, and attempts to retrofit 20th-century regulation onto 21st-century
media have produced regulatory creep. An authority originally meant to uphold stand‐
ards in scheduled broadcasting now routinely opines on expression, taste, and
decency in a fragmented digital environment.
This Bill recognises that the BSA model is no longer fit for purpose. It disestablishes
the Authority and removes associated statutory provisions from the Broadcasting Act
1989 and related enactments.
The Broadcasting (Disestablishment of Broadcasting Standards Authority) Amend‐
ment Bill is therefore a necessary modernisation. It clears away a legacy institution
that has outlived its usefulness, ensures consistency across all forms of media, and
affirms that New Zealanders can be trusted to make their own judgments in a free
society.
