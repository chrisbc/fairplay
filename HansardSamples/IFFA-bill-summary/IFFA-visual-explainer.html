🗺️ The Stakeholders

🏦 SPV Bondholders Wins
  • Statutory, levy-backed revenue stream
  • Equal ranking with rates in recovery
  • Forced sale powers if development fails
  • 30-year guaranteed income regardless of asset performance
🏗️ Infrastructure Developers Wins
  • Faster, cheaper approval process
  • Mandatory endorsements from councils
  • No affordability assessment for own land
  • Risk of project failure transferred to consumers via levy
💼 Investment Banks Wins
  • Arrangement fees on $B bond issuances
  • Zero exposure to stranding risk
  • Streamlined deal structures
  • No legal requirement to verify affordability
🏠 Homeowners Loses
  • Compulsory levy, no consultation
  • No affordability assessment
  • 30-year lock-in, no exit mechanism
  • Risk of forced land sale after 4 months non-payment
🔑 Renters Loses
  • Costs passed through by landlords
  • No voice in the levy process
  • No affordability protection
  • Often lower income; levy is regressive
🌿 Māori Landowners Loses
  • Consent bypassed for 5,000+ property projects
  • Biggest projects carry least protection
  • Treaty obligations not assessed (consultation repealed)
  • Land included without agreement
👶 Future Generations Loses
  • Inherit 30-year levy obligations
  • No say in decisions made before their time
  • May pay for stranded assets for decades
  • Intergenerational inequity baked in
🏙️ Councils Loses Control
  • Must endorse levies (discretion removed)
  • Rates now share equally with levies in recovery
  • Information-sharing obligations added
  • Water organisations gain new powers

🚦 What Can You Actually Do?

❌
Participate in consultation Section 28 consultation has been repealed entirely. There is no public participation process.
❌
Require an affordability assessment Exempt if the developer owns the land or gets "written support" from current owners — not future levypayers.
❌
Rely on your council to block it Councils must endorse if statutory requirements are met. The only permitted reason to refuse is if rate collection would be compromised.
❌
Stop paying if the asset becomes worthless The levy runs for the full 30-year period. No early termination mechanism exists. The levy continues even for stranded assets.
❌
Get a refund if too much is collected Excess levy is only assessed at the end of the levy period — not during the 30 years you are paying.
❌
Challenge the SPV's "failure" declaration The SPV self-certifies failure. A Registrar (not a judge) can then order the forced sale of land. The sale is valid even if irregular.
❌
See the full recommender's report OIA commercial confidentiality redactions are now permitted in published reports. You may not know the deal terms.
⚠️
Vote out the Minister Yes — but the levy order is already made. A new government faces the same legal constraint: no exit mechanism, and bondholder buyout costs billions.

📢 Key Messages

For homeowners
"You could be levied for 30 years for infrastructure you were never asked about, assessed for affordability, or given any say in — and there's no way out."
For renters
"Your landlord pays the levy. Your rent goes up. You had no voice in the decision and no protection against unaffordable charges."
For Māori communities
"The bigger the project, the less your consent matters. The 5,000-property threshold removes consent rights for exactly the developments with the greatest impact."
For voters
"One Minister, exercising fewer checks than ever, can commit your community to 30 years of compulsory charges — for infrastructure that could be a stranded asset within a decade."
For energy and climate
"A mechanism designed for suburban stormwater could fund a 30-year fossil fuel lock-in via your electricity bill — with no mechanism to stop it when renewables make it redundant."
The bottom line
"The winners designed the rules. The losers pay the bills. Nobody is accountable if it goes wrong."
