The Approval Chain

The Act 2020 required six independent gates before a levy order could be issued. The Amendment Bill 2025 eliminates every meaningful check except one Minister's personal judgement.

✅ Act 2020 — Six Independent Gates
1
Developer proposes levy
Full requirements: levy area, proposed infrastructure, eligible costs, protected Māori land details, affordability examples
Full proposal required
│
2
Infrastructure authority endorsement
Responsible authority may endorse — discretion retained. Can refuse for community or planning reasons.
Discretionary gate
│
3
Council (RLA) levy endorsement
Council may endorse. Can refuse based on broader community concerns. Rates rank first in any recovery.
Community safeguard
│
4
Recommender analysis
Must consider: long-term levypayer interests, affordability, sustainability of payment, Māori land impact
Full assessment
│
5
Minister's decision
Must consult Commerce, Consumer Finance, Local Govt, Rating, and Public Finance Ministers. Full criteria apply.
5-portfolio consultation
│
🏛️ Governor-General issues Levy Order
Backed by multi-portfolio review
❌ Bill 2025 — One Gatekeeper Remains
1
Developer proposes levy
Simplified requirements. If developer owns land: no Māori land consent needed (5,000+ properties), no affordability data required.
Reduced requirements
│
2
Infrastructure authority endorsement
Authority must endorse if statutory tests are met. Endorsement not even required for developer-owned infrastructure.
Mandatory — or bypassed
│
3
Council (RLA) levy endorsement
Council must endorse unless it can prove rate collection would be compromised — the only permitted ground for refusal. Rates now share equally with levy in recovery.
Community safeguard removed
│
4
Recommender analysis
Affordability and long-term levypayer interests are excluded from consideration if developer owns land or gets written support. Report may be commercially redacted.
Key criteria excluded
│
5
Minister's decision
Section 28 consultation repealed entirely. No other Minister need be consulted. Affordability excluded from mandatory considerations.
No consultation required
│
🏛️ Governor-General issues Levy Order
Backed by one Minister's personal judgement

The Net Effect

Act 2020
  • ✅ 5 Ministers consulted
  • ✅ Affordability assessed
  • ✅ Council can say no
  • ✅ Māori consent required
  • ✅ Full report published
  • ✅ 6 independent checks
Bill 2025
  • ❌ No consultation required
  • ❌ Affordability waived for developer land
  • ❌ Council must say yes
  • ❌ Māori consent bypassed at scale
  • ❌ Report may be redacted
  • ❌ 1 Minister decides alone

Analysis based on the Infrastructure Funding and Financing Act 2020 and Amendment Bill 2025
