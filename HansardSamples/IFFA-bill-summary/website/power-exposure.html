In a well-designed system, power and exposure would be aligned: those who make decisions would bear consequences, and those who bear consequences would have a voice in decisions. The amendment inverts this entirely — moving exposure to parties with no power, and power to parties with no exposure.

The Perverse Incentive Structure

Incentive to keep stranded assets running

SPV investors may lobby to keep infrastructure operating even when alternatives are cheaper — because a mothballed terminal with a running levy is politically toxic. Uneconomic operation maintains the narrative that the levy is justified.

Incentive against alternative investment

While the levy runs, every MWh from renewables still pays it. Investing in batteries or solar does not reduce the levy. The levy makes electricity more expensive, slowing electrification of transport and industry — the transition the levy was supposed to enable.

Incentive to overstate risks

If the IFF model "works" for one large project, it creates a template. Proponents have every incentive to overstate energy security risks to justify compulsory levy funding — especially with no affordability assessment, no consultation, and no council veto.
