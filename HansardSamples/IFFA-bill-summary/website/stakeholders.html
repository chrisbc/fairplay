The Stakeholders

Every party affected by the Amendment Bill — who gains protections and guaranteed income, and who loses safeguards, voice, and sometimes land.

🏦 SPV Bondholders Wins
  • Statutory, levy-backed revenue stream
  • Equal ranking with rates in recovery (up from behind rates)
  • Forced sale powers if development fails
  • 30-year guaranteed income regardless of asset performance
🏗️ Infrastructure Developers Wins
  • Faster, cheaper approval process
  • Mandatory endorsements from councils and infrastructure authorities
  • No affordability assessment required for own land
  • Risk of project failure transferred to consumers via the levy
💼 Investment Banks Wins
  • Arrangement fees on billion-dollar bond issuances
  • Zero exposure to stranding risk
  • Streamlined deal structures
  • No legal requirement to verify affordability for clients
🏠 Homeowners Loses
  • Compulsory levy, no consultation or notice
  • No affordability assessment for their specific situation
  • 30-year lock-in, no exit mechanism even if asset strands
  • Risk of forced land sale after 4 months non-payment
🔑 Renters Loses
  • Costs passed through by landlords as higher rent
  • No voice in the levy process whatsoever
  • No affordability protection despite being most vulnerable
  • Disproportionate burden — levy is flat, not income-related
🌿 Māori Landowners Loses
  • Consent bypassed for 5,000+ property projects — the biggest ones
  • Largest developments carry the least protection for Māori
  • Treaty obligations not assessed (consultation repealed)
  • Protected Māori land included in levy area without agreement
👶 Future Generations Loses
  • Inherit 30-year levy obligations made before they could participate
  • No say in infrastructure decisions affecting them longest
  • May pay for stranded assets for 20+ years without any benefit
  • Intergenerational inequity structurally embedded in the Act
🏙️ Councils / Ratepayers Loses Control
  • Must endorse levies — discretion to protect communities removed
  • Rates now rank equally with levies in recovery (previously ranked first)
  • New information-sharing obligations to SPVs
  • Water organisations gain new levy-raising powers

The Pattern

Those who win under the amended Act are the parties who designed the financing structure, hold the bonds, or earn fees upfront — entities that participate voluntarily and whose exposure to downside risk is either zero or contractually capped. Those who lose are the parties who never agreed to the arrangement, cannot opt out, receive no return for their risk, and have no legal mechanism to challenge continued payment even when the infrastructure no longer serves them.

Analysis based on the Infrastructure Funding and Financing Act 2020 and Amendment Bill 2025
